# ADR-032: Structural Audit Gate (Task 38.5)

**Status:** Accepted

## Context
Tasks 1–38 each produced a foundational component or framework — trading-domain frameworks, infrastructure frameworks like Exchange Adapters, and eventually the composition root itself. Task 38 specifically declares and wires **24** of those completed frameworks (`app/wiring.py:COMPONENT_REGISTRARS`) into a disposable dry-run container that proves the declared DI graph for those 24 wires. 38 tasks and 24 wired frameworks are not the same count, and this ADR does not conflate them: several Tasks 1–38 produced infrastructure (`core/`, `config/`, `events/`, `trading/`) or a framework not among the 24 wired ones (`exchange_adapters/`, Task 17 — see H-2 below), not one of the 24 `COMPONENT_REGISTRARS` entries. No task to date had re-examined the system *as a whole*, from real committed source rather than prior claims, for structural cracks that only become visible once every framework is considered together — cross-framework coupling no single framework's own review would catch, a safety boundary asserted in one task's docstring but never checked against a sibling's real behavior, or a trading-safety control the roadmap assumes exists but that no task actually built. `docs/prompts/task-38.5.md` specified a code-read-only audit to close that gap and, critically, to establish a gate: Task 39 should not begin work on top of an unverified foundation.

## Decision
Adopt a standing rule: **any open Critical-severity finding from a Task 38.5-class structural audit blocks the next dependent task from starting**, until each such finding is either resolved by a dedicated follow-up task or explicitly re-classified with a recorded justification. This gate is independent of what any particular audit run finds — it was decided as an architectural policy in `docs/prompts/task-38.5.md` before the audit ran, and this ADR records that policy as accepted regardless of outcome.

**Amendment, this closeout pass:** the policy above was written assuming the audit could conclusively determine whether a Critical finding exists. Five successive passes (v1–v5) of this audit each found something the previous pass's method could not see, which is itself evidence the trace has not converged on exhaustive. The policy is therefore amended to make explicit what it already implied: **if the audit cannot conclusively establish the absence of a Critical-severity path — as opposed to having discovered zero within what it walked — the gate is HOLD, not ALLOWED**, until either the assurance gap is closed by a stronger method or a deliberate, recorded decision accepts the residual risk. This is a distinct disposition from BLOCKED (which means a Critical finding *was* discovered); HOLD means the question was not conclusively answered either way.

**This run's outcome (honest closeout pass, 2026-08-20 — a document-only correction of language and gate disposition; the coverage evidence itself, gathered by the v5 patch pass, is unchanged and restated below):** the Task 38.5 audit (`docs/audits/task-38.5-structural-audit.md`, `docs/audits/task-38.5-risk-register.md`, `docs/audits/task-38.5-test-gaps.md`) records **0 Critical discovered, 2 High, 7 Medium, 9 Low** findings (v4 was 0/2/6/9; this pass adds **M-7**, naming the audit-assurance gap itself as a finding — no other count changed). **Gate outcome: INDETERMINATE / HOLD.** No forbidden trade, inference, network, DB, Redis, file, thread, or process path was found reachable from Task 38's own documented entrypoints (`app/main.py:main`, `app/bootstrap.py:run_dry_run_bootstrap`) *within the scope this audit's method actually covers* — confirmed by a recursive trace using 24 independent fresh containers, cross-checked by a static AST call inventory that walks `core.container.ServiceContainer`'s and `core.registry.ServiceRegistry`'s own methods, `app.planner.plan`, and `app.preflight.validate_service_keys`/`.run` as real, source-inspected targets, and resolves every `super().__init__()` call to its actual MRO base. **183** distinct real classes were reached and classified with **0 left unresolved** *by the classifier's own bookkeeping*; **155** unique calls were classified into six buckets with **0 unresolved** *by the same bookkeeping*; a module-state inventory classified 516 candidates with 0 unexplained *within its own scan's scope*; a runtime defence check patched a broad (not exhaustive — see M-7) I/O/lifecycle surface and `run_dry_run_bootstrap` still returned `SUCCESS` at `24/24`. **This is high-confidence coverage evidence, not a completeness proof** — see M-7 in the risk register for the four specific methodological gaps (`__post_init__` call-graph coverage; `os.open`/DB/Redis runtime-denial coverage; module-global mutation from a plain function; the residual text/name allowlist underlying "resolved"). **Task 39 must not begin on the strength of this audit alone; Task 38.6 — a source-controlled, reusable audit harness closing the four M-7 gaps — is the required follow-up before the gate is re-evaluated.** This is explicitly not the same thing as having found a Critical vulnerability: no such finding exists in this audit's record, at any severity above Medium.

The two High findings are real and are not waived by this decision: `app/wiring.py:3` and `docs/prompts/task-38.md:21`'s claim that "every prior framework is forbidden from importing another framework directly" is false for 14 of the 24 wired frameworks, plus `exchange_adapters` (which is not one of the 24 wired frameworks at all — a pre-existing architectural characteristic of the earlier trading-domain frameworks, not something Task 38 introduced or could have fixed within its own scope); and `COMPONENT_REGISTRARS`'s "all 24, every completed package" claim omits `exchange_adapters`, a fully-built Task 17 framework. Neither rises to Critical under the rubric below (neither opens a real I/O/trading/inference/credential-leak path), but both are recorded as open in the risk register and should be triaged by a near-term follow-up, not silently carried forward. **Both remain open and unimplemented after this pass**, exactly as before — this pass only corrects the audit's own language and gate disposition; it changed no code and does not resolve either finding.

**M-6 (Medium, open, not waived), refined in v5, unchanged by this pass:** `LoggerFactory.get_logger()` is called, guarded by `if logger else None`, at 46 distinct `__init__` sites (every `Manager` and `Engine` across the 24 wired frameworks). A default-configured `LoggerFactory` would attach a console handler (not a file) if reached — real file I/O (`os.makedirs`, `RotatingFileHandler`) additionally requires an explicit `file_path` configuration nothing in the current wiring sets. It is unreachable today, within verified scope, only because no registrar wires `LoggerFactory` into the dry-run graph — an application-level fact, not a structural guarantee a future registrar addition couldn't quietly break. See the risk register for full evidence and recommendation; not implemented here.

**M-7 (Medium, open, new in this pass):** the audit-assurance gap itself. Five passes each found a real gap in the previous pass's method; four specific, currently-open gaps remain (named above and in full in the risk register). This finding is the reason the gate is HOLD rather than ALLOWED. See the risk register for full evidence and the Task 38.6 recommendation; not implemented here.

## Alternatives Considered
- **No formal gate — treat the audit as informational only.** Rejected: an audit whose findings carry no consequence is easy to produce and easy to ignore; the entire point of running a structural audit before Task 39 is to make its outcome actionable.
- **Gate on any open finding, regardless of severity.** Rejected: with 9 Low findings already on record (mostly `baseline: unknown` typing gaps and test-coverage notes), gating on every open item would block indefinitely on cosmetic issues unrelated to safety. The severity rubric exists precisely so the gate tracks what actually matters — a real reachable I/O/trading/inference/credential-leak path.
- **Gate on High findings too.** Rejected for this run, but not dismissed as a future policy: the two High findings here are about the accuracy of an architectural *claim*, not about an unsafe path existing. Blocking Task 39 on a documentation-accuracy issue would be disproportionate; the risk register's disposition (`Open`) already ensures they aren't forgotten.
- **Re-run the audit until 0 High findings too, before allowing Task 39.** Rejected: this would make audit accuracy — finding real, pre-existing issues — a reason not to ship the audit, incentivizing under-reporting. The gate is about newly-tolerable risk (Critical), not about the audit finding nothing.
- **Treat "0 Critical findings discovered" as equivalent to "0 Critical findings exist" and leave the gate ALLOWED.** Rejected in this closeout pass: five successive passes each found a real gap the last one's method missed, which is direct evidence against treating the current pass as the last word. Conflating "discovered none" with "none exist" would make the gate's own Critical-only criterion unfalsifiable — it would always read ALLOWED regardless of how much of the codebase the method actually covered, which defeats the purpose of having a gate at all.
- **Re-run yet another ad hoc `/tmp` script (a "v6") to try to close the gap immediately.** Rejected for this pass, per its own explicit instruction: the v1–v5 pattern of one-off scripts each fixing the previous one's blind spot is itself part of the problem M-7 names — another one-off script would likely find one more gap and leave the same open-ended uncertainty, not close it. The gap needs a source-controlled, reusable harness (Task 38.6), not another iteration of the same throwaway-script method.

## Consequences
### Positive
- Task 39's eventual start (once the gate is re-evaluated) will rest on a documented, evidence-based, independently-derived, and honestly-scoped confirmation of Task 38's dry-run boundary, rather than an assumption carried forward from Task 38's own completion report or from an overclaimed "complete" audit.
- Two real architectural-claim inaccuracies (H-1, H-2) are now on record with exact evidence, instead of remaining implicit and un-discoverable until someone happens to read all 24 frameworks' imports by hand.
- Seven Medium trading-safety/I-O/assurance gaps (five from v3, M-6 from v4, M-7 from this pass) are now an explicit, evidence-backed inventory a future task can triage against, rather than something that task would have to rediscover from scratch.
- The v4 pass corrected a real completeness gap in v3's own method (15 `Engine` classes it could not see) and closed Area 1's narrow-scope caveat on module-level shared state; the v5 pass then corrected v4's own remaining allowlist-by-text shortcuts. This closeout pass names, rather than lets the reader infer, that the pattern of "each pass finds what the last one missed" is itself the finding (M-7) — which is a more honest position than treating the fifth correction as necessarily the last one needed.
- The gate policy itself (Critical-only, now amended to also HOLD on an unresolved assurance gap) is precedent for future structural audits, so the next one doesn't have to re-litigate what severity — or what degree of unresolved uncertainty — should block what.

### Negative
- **Task 39 is blocked pending Task 38.6**, not merely "tracked with open findings" — this is a stronger consequence than the prior ALLOWED-with-open-findings disposition, and is the direct cost of this pass's honesty correction.
- The two High findings remain unresolved; a future task must still decide whether to correct the documentation claim, migrate the older frameworks toward DI-only isolation, or accept the difference as intentional and document it as such.
- The seven Medium trading-safety/I-O/assurance gaps remain unimplemented by design (this audit explicitly forbids implementing anything it finds missing) — whatever eventually follows must not assume they've been silently closed.
- `exchange_adapters`' exclusion from the composition root (H-2) means it continues to be un-covered by the 24/24 preflight check until a follow-up task adds it.

## Safety Boundaries
This ADR records a gate decision only — it makes no code change and asserts no new safety boundary itself. The safety boundaries it relies on (no forbidden I/O/trading/inference/credential-leak path reachable from `app/main.py:main`/`app/bootstrap.py:run_dry_run_bootstrap`, *within verified scope*) are the ones independently re-verified in `docs/audits/task-38.5-structural-audit.md` Area 3 (and Area 3b for module-level shared state) — themselves now explicitly framed as a high-confidence bounded trace, not a completeness proof. This ADR does not assert those boundaries hold repo-wide beyond what that trace actually covers.
